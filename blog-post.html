5 Simple Techniques to Manage Anxiety in Daily Life

15 March 2026 Dr. Sarah Mitchell
Replace with featured image
(blog-hero.jpg — 1200×675px)

Anxiety is one of the most common mental health challenges people face today. While it's a natural response to stress, persistent anxiety can significantly impact your daily life, relationships, and overall wellbeing. The good news is that there are practical, evidence-based techniques you can start using right away.

1. Grounding with the 5-4-3-2-1 Technique

When anxiety strikes, your mind often races ahead to worst-case scenarios. The 5-4-3-2-1 technique brings you back to the present moment by engaging all five senses. Notice five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear, two you can smell, and one you can taste.

2. Box Breathing

Box breathing is a simple but powerful tool used even by military personnel in high-stress situations. Breathe in for four counts, hold for four counts, breathe out for four counts, and hold again for four counts. Repeat this cycle four to six times whenever you feel anxious.

The greatest weapon against stress is our ability to choose one thought over another.

3. Thought Journaling

Writing down anxious thoughts can help you identify patterns and challenge unhelpful thinking. Try keeping a simple journal where you note the situation, your automatic thought, the emotion it triggers, and an alternative perspective.

4. Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Anxiety often manifests physically — tension in your shoulders, a tight jaw, or a churning stomach. Progressive muscle relaxation involves deliberately tensing and then releasing each muscle group in your body, helping you recognise and release physical tension.

5. Setting Boundaries with Worry Time

Rather than trying to suppress anxious thoughts entirely, designate a specific 15-minute window each day as your "worry time". When anxious thoughts arise outside this window, acknowledge them and postpone them to your scheduled time. Many people find that by the time worry time arrives, the concerns feel less urgent.

When to Seek Professional Support

These techniques are excellent starting points, but if anxiety is significantly interfering with your daily life, work, or relationships, it may be time to speak with a professional. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) has strong evidence for treating anxiety disorders and can provide you with a personalised toolkit for managing your specific challenges.
